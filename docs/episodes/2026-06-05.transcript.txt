Policy & Regulation Watch: Florida sues OpenAI and Sam Altman personally treating ChatGPT as a defective product

In a historic move, Florida has filed an 83-page lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman, treating ChatGPT as a defective product. This is the first time a U.S. state has applied product liability and public nuisance laws to an AI chatbot.

If successful, this lawsuit could open the floodgates for similar legal actions across the nation, potentially reshaping the entire AI landscape. This could impose new liabilities on AI developers that they’ve never faced before.

However, the legal framework for product liability in AI is still largely uncharted territory. Significant hurdles remain, and it’s uncertain whether this case will survive early challenges in court.

Policy & Regulation Watch: New York legislature passes first statewide one-year ban on new large data centers

In a notable legislative move, New York has passed a bill imposing a one-year moratorium on new large data centers. If signed into law, this would be the first statewide ban in the U.S., potentially influencing other states to follow suit.

This moratorium could significantly impact the expansion timelines and costs for cloud providers, especially those heavily invested in AI infrastructure. It's a clear signal of growing concerns over the environmental impact of such data centers.

However, the bill still needs Governor Hochul's signature, and if she does sign it, legal challenges from the data-center industry are likely. So, while this is a big step, its future is uncertain.
