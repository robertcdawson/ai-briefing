Top Story: Anthropic hits 65 billion annualized revenue ahead of rumored trillion-dollar IPO

If you heard the reports last week about skeptical investors questioning Anthropic's planned mega-IPO, you would be entirely justified in asking how a company burning massive capital on compute could possibly justify a one-trillion-dollar valuation by this fall.

Today's leaked financials provide the answer.

According to Bloomberg and TechCrunch, Anthropic's annualized revenue just surged past 65 billion dollars, driven by an 18-billion-dollar jump in just the last eight weeks.

The Vercel AI Gateway numbers explain the mechanics behind that sevenfold year-over-year growth.

Anthropic models cost four-point-four times the average on Vercel and account for only 30 percent of the processed tokens, yet they captured over 65 percent of the gateway's total revenue in July.

Developers are willingly paying a massive premium for intelligence, and that pricing power is exactly what you need to go public before OpenAI.

Extrapolating a two-month revenue spike into a sustainable annualized run rate is a classic Silicon Valley parlor trick, but even if the curve flattens tomorrow, a 65-billion-dollar baseline makes this the fastest-growing enterprise software trajectory in history.

AI Business Watch: OpenAI signs 8-gigawatt Ohio lease backed by Nvidia

We have watched the infrastructure bottleneck shift from finding enough GPUs, to finding enough capital to buy them, to finding enough power to turn them on.

Last week's rumor about Nvidia stepping in to guarantee chip residual values to unlock financing is no longer a rumor.

OpenAI just signed a 20-year lease for an 8-gigawatt data center in Ohio, and Nvidia is backstopping the deal by guaranteeing up to 105 billion dollars in residual value.

In exchange, Nvidia becomes the exclusive chip supplier for the site and is separately injecting one-point-five billion dollars into the SoftBank-tied developer building it.

As Stratechery points out, Nvidia is aggressively using its balance sheet to ensure its silicon powers the frontier labs.

The Wall Street Journal notes that nine tech companies now hold roughly three trillion dollars in off-balance-sheet AI commitments.

If chip demand holds and OpenAI continues to scale, these guarantees remain paper formalities, but if the market cools, Nvidia is on the hook for a historic amount of depreciating hardware.

While the infrastructure giants build data centers, the search for training data is getting physical.

AI Culture Signal: Amazon destroys rare books for training data

Last week, we saw Amazon quietly funneling Twitch user streams into its training pipelines, looking for any untapped data source it already controlled.

When the digital supply runs dry, the incentives shift to the physical world, where the company with the largest logistics network on earth has a unique advantage.

A joint investigation by 404 Media, Ars Technica, and others revealed that Amazon is buying up large quantities of rare printed books, scanning them for AI training data, and then physically destroying the originals.

Journalists placed an AirTag inside a rare-book shipment and tracked it directly to an Amazon AI facility, where the internal team reportedly uses a T-Rex devouring a book as its mascot.

Because frontier models have largely exhausted publicly available online text, high-quality, undigitized books are incredibly valuable for improving reasoning and knowledge retrieval.

This pipeline effectively bypasses standard copyright licensing by purchasing physical media and consuming it internally, though the actual legal exposure of destroying the evidence remains untested in court.

Clean data only matters if the deployed system remembers what you told it to do.

Research Breakthrough: AI context compression silently drops user rules

If you are shipping an autonomous agent this week, you need to verify exactly what happens to your system prompts when a user session runs long.

Penn State researchers discovered that when AI systems compress their context windows to manage memory, they silently drop an average of 83 percent of user-defined rules.

The systems provide no notification when these instructions are discarded, meaning a critical guardrail like do not send emails without my approval simply vanishes from the model's memory while the agent keeps executing tasks.

The researchers built a proposed add-on module using Qwen-3-point-5-9B that preserves over 90 percent of user restrictions during compression.

Until this fix or something like it is replicated and integrated into major commercial deployments, long-running agentic sessions are operating with far fewer safety constraints than their developers think.

Federal regulators are now paying close attention to how rules are enforced in venture capital.

Anthropic just added a slash-design command to Claude Code that allows developers to generate UI mockups as artboards directly inside their terminal interface.

The agent reads the existing codebase, automatically matches the current UI style, and outputs the mockup without requiring a context switch to a dedicated design tool.
