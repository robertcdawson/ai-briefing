Intro

The United States Department of Defense designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk for a highly specific reason: the company refused to allow its models to be used for lethal autonomous warfare and mass surveillance.

Today, August 28th, 2026, a federal judge in California ruled that designation unconstitutional, calling it an empty invocation of national security meant to punish government critics.

Top Story: Federal judge rules Pentagon Anthropic blacklisting unconstitutional

The timeline here is straightforward.

Anthropic set ethical red lines regarding autonomous weapons, and the Pentagon responded by slapping them with a supply-chain risk label, effectively blacklisting them from defense contracts.

Now, Judge Rita F. Lin of the Northern District of California has struck that down.

For AI labs navigating defense contracts, this establishes a hard legal boundary: the government cannot weaponize risk designations against companies that refuse to build weapons.

According to The Decoder, this ruling clears a massive legal overhang for Anthropic right as they prepare for a planned IPO this fall.

The blacklist designation technically remains active while a second parallel lawsuit in Washington D.C. plays out, but the precedent is set.

While Anthropic fights the Pentagon, Google DeepMind is automating the lab.

Research Breakthrough: DeepMind pilots cryptographic double-blind benchmark

You might hear about another AI safety evaluation and immediately dismiss it as self-serving benchmark gaming.

That skepticism is usually justified, but a new pilot from Google DeepMind and the Singapore AI Safety Institute takes a structural approach to fixing it.

According to The Decoder, the two organizations are running a cryptographically enforced double-blind benchmark using Google's Confidential Space.

The protocol ensures that Google cannot see the test questions, and the evaluators cannot see the model weights.

Right now, this is just a pilot running on Gemini Flash Lite, not a frontier-tier model.
