Top Story: AI slowdown pact draws cartel accusations

The companies pushing hardest for a coordinated pause on frontier model development are the ones who already have frontier models.

Cohere CEO Aidan Gomez looked at those incentives and called the initiative a cartel by another name, designed to shut out competitors who are still training to catch up.

Yesterday we noted that Dario Amodei's pacing proposal lacked any enforcement mechanism, making it a rhetorical exercise.

That take held up, but the situation has now escalated into a geopolitical flashpoint.

Jensen Huang told Donald Trump onstage at the All-In Summit that they are simply not going to let an AI slowdown happen.

Trump backed him up on a live speakerphone call, dismissing AI job displacement fears as a hoax and assuring the crowd that the robots will not be taking over.

Meanwhile, China's Foreign Ministry labeled the United States safety warnings as fearmongering meant to lock in American technological supremacy, with state media citing a silent AI Cold War.

In the middle of this friction, Microsoft published a 37-page humanist AI code of conduct declaring that people matter more than AI.

Any coordinated regulatory slowdown is dead on arrival when the world's largest chipmaker, a sitting US president, and the Chinese government all publicly oppose it.

Product & Tool Watch: Apple ships rebuilt Siri on Google Gemini

For developers integrating with Siri this morning, your prompts are now being processed by Google's model stack.

Apple just released iOS 27, featuring a completely rebuilt Siri that runs partly on-device and partly through Apple's Private Cloud Compute infrastructure.

According to a report from The Decoder, the backend relies heavily on Google Gemini, a detail Apple has largely kept out of its own marketing materials.

Last week, we predicted Apple's hardware-level privacy isolation would become the industry baseline.

That remains true for local processing, but offloading complex queries to Gemini introduces entirely new privacy architecture questions for developers handling sensitive user data.

Early reviews are positive, with one TechCrunch writer noting they are actually using Siri again after years of the assistant feeling limited.

We are also seeing new developer surfaces open up, like the fashion app Daydream allowing users to search outfit photos via Siri without even opening the application.

The major limitation is that the European Union is excluded from this launch entirely, strictly capping the addressable market.

While Apple changes its architecture, OpenAI is facing serious questions about your prompt data.

Policy & Regulation Watch: Contractors reading ChatGPT conversations

Default-on data sharing is a massive liability for enterprise deployment, and OpenAI's current setup appears to be exactly that.

According to leaked internal documents obtained by 404 Media, hundreds of OpenAI contract workers are actively reading and rating real ChatGPT conversations on a scale of one to seven.

The program is reportedly called Project Lily, and its stated goal is to reduce model flattery and human-like behavior.

The core issue is that the setting to improve the model for everyone is enabled by default, meaning users have to actively dig into their preferences to opt out.

While the prompts are supposedly anonymized, 404 Media reports they regularly contain highly sensitive personal and corporate information.

OpenAI has not publicly confirmed the scale of this program or the Project Lily name, so we are relying on these leaked documents.

Protecting your data takes vigilance, much like verifying a million-dollar mathematical breakthrough.

Research Breakthrough: Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem

OpenAI claimed a mathematical breakthrough last week, but researchers immediately accused them of using stolen unpublished work.

Now, the institution that actually hands out the awards is weighing in.

The Clay Mathematics Institute issued a statement saying the Navier-Stokes problem, one of seven Millennium Prize Problems worth one million dollars, has apparently been settled.

We noted last week that the misconduct allegations against OpenAI would be the defining story if verified.

That question remains open, but the actual proof might be legitimate regardless of how it was obtained.

The Institute is only saying it is apparently settled, and a formal review is underway.

No prize has been awarded yet, and independent peer review is incomplete.

Proving that math takes time, just like proving an AI lab is actually profitable.

AI Business Watch: Anthropic eyes Nasdaq IPO

When a frontier AI lab claims it is turning a profit, your immediate question should be what expenses they are hiding to make the math work.

Anthropic just told investors it expects a second straight profitable quarter and is targeting a Nasdaq IPO, according to a report from The Decoder.

The catch is that their profit figure excludes stock-based compensation.

For a company at Anthropic's valuation and headcount, equity is a massive line item.

If they do list on the Nasdaq, they would be the first major frontier lab to go public, and that liquidity event would force them to disclose their true financials.

Until they file public paperwork, we are looking at adjusted metrics designed to win over investors, making it nearly impossible to assess their actual unit economics.

While Anthropic focuses on Wall Street, OpenAI is quietly buying up hardware talent.

AI Business Watch: OpenAI acquires Glass Imaging

OpenAI started with web interfaces, moved into voice interactions, and has aggressively pursued device partnerships for the last year.

Owning the visual input layer was the obvious next step.

TechCrunch reports, citing unnamed sources, that OpenAI is acquiring smartphone camera maker Glass Imaging for about 300 million dollars.

Glass Imaging was founded by former Apple engineers who led the development of the iPhone's Portrait Mode.

Controlling their own camera pipelines means OpenAI won't have to rely on third-party hardware to feed richer multimodal inputs into their models.

OpenAI hasn't officially confirmed the deal or announced how they plan to integrate the team.
