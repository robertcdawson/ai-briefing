Top Story: Single prompt could hijack every AI agent in an AWS Bedrock account

If you manage agent fleets on AWS, your default permissions are significantly tighter this morning than they were last week.

Security researchers found a way to use a single text prompt to take over any AI agent in a Bedrock account.

The attack worked by exploiting an internal AWS interface that handed out temporary cloud credentials without checking who was actually asking for them.

Amazon has closed the specific attack vector and locked down those default permissions.

The broader problem is that multi-agent architectures on managed cloud platforms carry blast-radius risks that simple single-model deployments avoid.

When your agents talk to each other and share access tokens, one compromised node can compromise the whole network.

We do not know if similar credential loopholes exist on competing cloud platforms.

While developers patch those cloud vulnerabilities, OpenAI is trying to patch a massive hole in its own financial projections.

Research Breakthrough: Claude Science AI agents produce first complete ultraviolet map of the entire sky

The strange detail about the first complete ultraviolet map of the sky is not the map itself.

It is the fact that astrophysicist Brice Ménard at Johns Hopkins University says the project simply would not have gotten done without AI.

His team used Anthropic's Claude Science to deploy AI agents that downloaded data from multiple space missions and calibrated the instruments.

The agents then filled in missing gaps using a technique called inpainting, where the model predicts missing pixels based on surrounding patterns.

Those predictions averaged about a ten percent deviation from actual measurements.

This gives builders a concrete benchmark for what agentic science workflows can actually accomplish when humans step back and let the system run.

The ten percent deviation figure has not been peer-reviewed yet, and independent teams still need to replicate the result.

While agents are mapping the stars, the people supposed to be keeping them safe are fighting over why they lost their jobs.

Product & Tool Watch: Google launches universal Gemini enterprise agent and offline meeting note-taker

Russia's Dark Clark campaign just proved how cheap that trade has become by using AI to spread disinformation across Latin America.

The operation successfully drew reactions from regional politicians, earning OpenAI's first-ever Category 5 out of 6 rating in its threat reporting history.

OpenAI banned all accounts associated with both the Russian operation and a separate Iranian campaign.

A Category 5 rating signals that AI-assisted disinformation has crossed a threshold of real-world political impact.
