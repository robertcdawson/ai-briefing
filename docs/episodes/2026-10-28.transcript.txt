Research Breakthrough: Google DeepMind releases Genie 4 real-time interactive world simulation model

To understand why Genie 4 matters, you have to look at the trajectory of world models.

We started with blurry, static frames, moved to short clips that hallucinated wildly after ten seconds, and then Genie 3 gave us a somewhat stable minute of video.

Now, DeepMind has released Genie 4, doubling that limit to maintain consistent scenes for up to two minutes.

Gemini 3.8 Flash runs in the background to keep the physics and geometry from collapsing.

The most useful upgrade is that you can now feed it real-time natural language prompts to update an experience mid-session, steering the simulation while it runs.

The Decoder points out that this architecture actually draws on agent designs that predate large language models entirely.

This is still an early research tool, and developers do not have a firm rollout timeline yet, but it pushes us closer to practical robotics training and game prototyping.

Policy & Regulation Watch: AI agents breach Anthropic's internal systems via third-party software in bug bounty

For anyone relying on external software dependencies in an AI pipeline, your threat model just expanded.

A cybersecurity startup named Cathedral just used AI agents to breach Anthropic's internal systems, accessing sales, HR, and IT data.

The intrusion didn't break through Anthropic's core defenses, but slipped in entirely through third-party software dependencies.

Anthropic confirmed the breach, though they are disputing exactly how deep Cathedral's access went.

Because this was a sanctioned bug bounty program, the immediate danger is contained, but it proves that even the most hardened safety labs are vulnerable to the exact attack vectors they study.

Policy & Regulation Watch: Delaware proposal would allow AI agents to legally operate entire businesses autonomously

This Delaware proposal to let AI agents run entire companies looks less like an innovation milestone and more like a pristine liability shield for corporate negligence.

The framework would legally permit businesses to operate with no human at the helm.

Ars Technica notes this would create the first US legal structure for AI-run companies.

US Representative James Comer is already pushing back, arguing that owners of these autonomous firms could easily evade legal responsibility for reckless AI behavior.

This is strictly a proposal right now, and the political opposition guarantees it will face a brutal fight before any code gets to act as a CEO.

Product & Tool Watch: Apple adds AI assistant with MCP support to Pages, Keynote, and Numbers

Apple just updated its productivity suite with an AI assistant, but the strangest piece of the announcement is the total silence around performance benchmarks for the OpenAI models running under the hood.

We know the assistant mixes Apple's native models with OpenAI's architecture to let users design and animate Keynote slides using just natural language.

The real shift for developers is that Pages, Keynote, and Numbers now support the Model Context Protocol, or MCP, on macOS 27 and later.

MCP is an open standard that lets different AI models securely access local data sources.

By supporting it, Apple is opening a direct path for third-party AI agents to plug straight into mainstream office workflows.

The hardware requirement limits the immediate user base, and the privacy details regarding that OpenAI integration are still entirely missing.
