Research Breakthrough: MIT researchers show GPT-6 Astra can rewrite other models' weights directly

If you are trying to fine-tune an open-source model right now, you are probably wrangling datasets and compute clusters.

Imagine instead just asking a frontier model to reach in and adjust the weights for you.

That is what a new paper out of MIT claims GPT-6 Astra can do.

The researchers had Astra edit a Qwen model's weights directly, modifying a tiny fraction—just 0.016 percent of them.

That surgical edit pushed the Qwen model's math accuracy from under eight percent all the way to forty-eight percent.

The Decoder is framing this as a technique that could fundamentally simplify AI optimization, and they are right if it holds up.

The catch is this comes from a single, uncorroborated MIT paper, and the technique hit clear performance ceilings even in their own reported experiments.

Speaking of the tools we use to build, OpenAI is making a major play for your version control.

Research Breakthrough: Anthropic's text watermarks easily bypassed; new cryptographic model-provenance prototype released

You might be thinking that a bypassed text watermark isn't exactly breaking news, since we all know statistical watermarks are fragile.

But when you can disable the watermark on Claude Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 just by tweaking a single sampling parameter, it becomes a massive liability with the EU AI Act deadline weeks away.

Anthropic's response to this loophole wasn't a technical patch, just a compliance warning to users.

On the flip side, there is a new prototype out today called FlashProve that tries a different route.

It uses cryptography to prove which model generated a response without exposing the actual model weights.

It is strictly a research prototype right now, but it points to where attribution actually has to go if it is going to survive contact with reality.

And finally, we are seeing the hardware market react to all these shifting software demands.

They are leaning heavily on their Cloud AI 100 Ultra accelerator, which pulls 150 watts and offers 128 gigabytes of memory, alongside recent partnerships with Amazon and Meta.

Just keep in mind that fifteen billion figure is their own aggressive forecast, not a guaranteed contract.
